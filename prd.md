Here's the full brainstorm, grouped.
Visual representations (contact sheets for sound)

Multi-resolution spectrogram sheets. Tile mel spectrograms at three zoom levels into one image: transient scale (~50ms windows), bar scale, and whole-song scale. Vision models read spectrograms surprisingly well; a distorted 808 vs a clean sub is visually obvious, as are hi-hat density, reverb tails, and lowpass sweeps.
Self-similarity matrices. Render the song against itself as a similarity heatmap. Choruses appear as visible blocks, repeats as diagonals. An LLM can literally see arrangement structure, whether sections contrast, and where transitions land. Massively underused for LLM consumption.
Stem masking heatmaps. Time x frequency image where color intensity = how much stem A's energy overlaps stem B's. The kick/bass fight becomes a literal red blob at 60-90Hz on bars 5-8.
Per-band stereo width plots + goniometer snapshots. Width by frequency band over time, plus correlation. "Your low end is too wide and will collapse in mono" becomes a picture.
Onset rasters. A piano-roll-style grid of every stem's onsets stacked vertically. Rhythmic interlock vs rhythmic mud is a spatial pattern; syncopation and pocket are visible.
Modulation spectrograms (a spectrogram of the envelope, not the signal). This is one of the most underexplored representations in existence. Modulation energy at 2-8Hz encodes groove and rhythmic feel; 20-150Hz encodes growl and roughness (this is where dubstep basses actually live). Two basses with near-identical FFTs can have wildly different modulation spectra, and that difference is most of the perceived "movement."
Cochlear excitation patterns (Bark/ERB-scale). Render what the ear receives after auditory filtering rather than the raw FFT. Two waveforms that excite the cochlea identically are perceptually the same sound, and the raw spectrum hides this.
Tension curves. A single composite line (dissonance + loudness + onset density + register) over time. Good songs have recognizable shapes; you can compare a track's tension curve against genre archetypes the way you'd compare a plot arc.

Measuring adjectives (the "fatter kick" problem)

Loudness-match first, always. "Fatter" is about 60% "louder." Normalize both kicks to identical LUFS before any comparison or every judgment downstream is contaminated. Deterministic, cheap, non-negotiable.
A producer-adjective lexicon compiled to feature math. Fat = high sub ratio (30-90Hz vs total) + moderate harmonic distortion + longer decay. Punchy = high crest factor + steep attack slope + short decay. Boxy = 250-500Hz bump. Harsh = energy at 2-5kHz + high sharpness. Ship it as a dictionary the analyzer evaluates, then calibrate the thresholds once against human A/B labels.
Actual psychoacoustic metrics. Zwicker/Fastl loudness (sones), sharpness (acum), roughness (asper), fluctuation strength, tonality. The MoSQITo Python library computes these today. This is the closest existing thing to "numbers for feel," and it's used heavily in automotive sound design and almost nowhere in music tooling.
Differential description, never absolute. Describe pairs as deltas: "Kick A: +5dB at 50Hz, 12ms slower attack, 2x the harmonic distortion of Kick B." LLMs, like humans, are far better at relative judgments than absolute ones. Your kick question resolves cleanly here: compute the delta vector, translate through the lexicon, answer "A is fatter, B is punchier" with receipts.
Embedding-space anchor libraries. Curate a few hundred reference sounds with rich human descriptions, embed everything with CLAP or MERT, and describe any new sample by its nearest anchors: "87% toward '90s boom-bap kick,' 40% toward '808 sub.'" The anchor library becomes a shared vocabulary between you and the model.
Timbre decomposition. HPSS-split any sample into transient and sustain, then measure each separately: attack time, spectral centroid trajectory, decay curve, odd/even harmonic ratio (warmth vs hollowness). Also detect the kick's fundamental so it can be tuned to the key deterministically, which fixes more amateur tracks than any EQ move.

Interaction and clash (the mixing problem)

Repurpose MP3 masking math. The psychoacoustic model inside every lossy codec answers "does sound A render sound B inaudible" and has since the 90s. Run it between stems instead of within one signal: "bass masks kick by 7dB in Bark band 4, bars 5-8" is a compiler error for mixes. This is the single most underexplored repurposing I know of.
Bark-band collision matrices per stem pair. Shows which instruments fight and where, and whether the fix is EQ carving or arrangement (just don't play them simultaneously).
Spectral dissonance (Plomp-Levelt, Sethares). Compute roughness from the actual spectra of simultaneous sounds, not note names. Theory says the notes are consonant; the two detuned saws may still beat horribly. Sethares made dissonance a computable function of timbre, which also explains why a chord works on one patch and dies on another.
Auditory scene analysis predictors (Bregman). Compute whether two layered sounds will fuse into one percept or stay separate: onset synchrony within ~5ms, harmonic alignment, co-modulation. This is the entire question in kick/bass layering and it's essentially untouched as a tool.
Phase and polarity checks for layers. Cross-correlate layered samples, report lag and polarity, auto-align. Half of "these sound worse together" is destructive interference, and it's 100% deterministic.
Ducking analysis. Cross-correlate envelopes to detect or prescribe sidechain relationships: "bass ducks 4dB under each kick, 90ms release" is a checkable spec, not a vibe.

Song as code

A textual intermediate representation for production. MidiTrain decompiles audio to MIDI; extend the philosophy to a full production decompiler: tempo map, key, per-stem sample IDs, synth patches as parameter sets, FX chains with settings, automation as breakpoints, arrangement grid. The song becomes a diffable, version-controlled document the LLM edits, and a renderer compiles back to audio.
Write the song in an actual audio language. Faust, SuperCollider, ChucK, Strudel/TidalCycles, Sonic Pi. LLMs are strongest at code; making music a code artifact gets you determinism, diffs, and reproducibility for free.
Headless DAW as the compiler. DawDreamer (a Python-embedded DAW engine that hosts real VSTs) or Reaper via ReaScript renders the IR offline with no GUI. This is the linchpin that makes the whole loop runnable by Claude Code today.
Constraint solvers for theory. Voice leading and counterpoint have been formal rule systems since Fux in 1725. LLM states intent ("gospel-ish reharm, soprano under E5"), an OR-tools-style solver guarantees zero parallel fifths and smooth voicings. Theory correctness becomes SAT, not vibes.

The quality loop (making it actually sound good)

Render, analyze, revise: exactly code + tests. LLM writes the IR, renderer compiles, an analysis suite emits a report card (LUFS curve, masking matrix, dissonance, stereo, structure, embedding distance to references), LLM reads the report and patches. The model never needs to hear anything, the same way Claude Code never executes code in its head; it needs actionable failure messages.
Reference matching as the objective function. Pick three reference tracks, extract target curves (spectral tilt, dynamics, width by band, onset density per section, section lengths). "Good" becomes "within tolerance of the reference profile," which is literally what mastering engineers do by ear.
A mix linter with named rules. MONO_LOW_END, KICK_BASS_MASKING, MUD_BUDGET (200-500Hz energy cap), HARSHNESS_CEILING, CREST_FACTOR_RANGE per bus, TUNED_KICK. Deterministic pass/fail with error messages phrased as fixes. Genre presets swap the thresholds.
Genre style sheets. A genre as machine-readable spec: BPM range, swing %, arrangement template, spectral tilt target, palette embedding centroid, density curves. CSS for music; the LLM fills the template, the linter enforces it.
Groove templates. Extract microtiming and velocity deviations from human performances (you have decades of your own playing as source data), store as reusable grooves, apply deterministically. Quantized-but-alive becomes a data problem.
Musical regression tests. Same seed + same spec = same render, so change one thing and diff the analysis report: "this EQ move cut masking 4dB and didn't move reference distance." Code review for mixes.
A critic ensemble with rubrics, not one mushy score. Hook memorability proxies (melodic n-gram self-similarity, interval distribution, singable range), arrangement contrast (does the drop measurably differ from the build in energy, density, width), genre fit (embedding distance). Separate numbers per axis.
Human preference calibration at scale. You have 1.27M subscribers; 15-second A/B polls calibrate the adjective lexicon and linter thresholds with real ears at a scale most labs would envy. The subjective layer becomes measured data instead of hand-waving.
Energy budgeting for arrangement. Allocate spectral and dynamic budget per section (intro spends 40% of full-band energy, drop spends 100%) so contrast is planned numerically rather than hoped for.
Audio novelty detection, mirroring PySceneDetect. Spectral flux novelty curves give you "scene cuts" in music: useful for decompiling references and validating your own arrangement's transitions.
Specialist agent fleet over the shared IR. Drummer, Bassist, Harmony, Arranger, MixEngineer, Mastering, Critic, each owning its analysis tools and negotiating changes to the same document. A natural OpenClaw formation.

The synthesis: "sounds good" decomposes into three layers. Correctness (no masking, mono-safe, tuned, in-pocket) is fully deterministic via the linter. Style conformance (matches reference profiles and genre sheets) is semi-deterministic. Taste is the residual, and it gets handled by calibrated human preference data plus a critic rubric. The first two layers get you from "obviously AI slop" to "professionally competent," which is 90% of the gap, and taste rides on top of a solid floor instead of trying to substitute for one. There's a McGilchrist shape to this you'll appreciate: the whole engine is a left-hemisphere scaffold of explicit measurement built to approximate right-hemisphere gestalt judgment, with an actual ear as the final arbiter.
If I were sequencing the build: DawDreamer render harness first, then the report card v1 (librosa + Essentia + MoSQITo + the masking model), then the lexicon calibrated with a weekend of your own A/Bs, then the linter, then hand the loop to Claude Code.
