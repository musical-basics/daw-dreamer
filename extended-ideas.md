What you built in MidiTrain is two instances of a general move, and naming it reveals the whole design space. The color mapping is state compression: fold a 12-D chroma state into a low-dimensional trackable signal so that change becomes computable as distance. The thermodynamic model is a latent dynamical variable: raw events (onsets, notes) become injections into a state that has memory, and the musically meaningful stuff lives in the derivatives of that state, not the events themselves. Feel is almost never in the first-order signal. It's in motion, force, and expectation. So the recipe is: pick a musically meaningful state, compress it, differentiate it, and compare it against a prediction.
Wiring your two frameworks into the DawDreamer engine

Color runs on both sides of the render, which gives you a free validation loop. Compute harmonic color from the IR's MIDI (exact) and from the rendered audio's chromagram (NNLS chroma works well). If they diverge, the timbre is distorting the harmony: a detuned supersaw or heavily distorted bass smearing pitch content the score doesn't have. "Timbral harmonic distortion" as a lint rule is, as far as I know, genuinely novel.
Upgrade hex distance to perceptual distance. Convert your color space to CIELAB and use ΔE (CIEDE2000) between windows as harmonic novelty. Color science spent 50 years calibrating perceptual distance; borrow it wholesale. If your pitch-class-to-hue mapping follows the circle of fifths (as Scriabin's clavier à lumières roughly did), hue distance approximates key distance and ΔE becomes a psychoacoustically-flavored modulation detector.
Saturation = tonal clarity. Blend the colors of all sounding pitch classes weighted by salience: a clean triad gives a crisp saturated blend, a chromatic cluster gives mud. Saturation over time is a tonal ambiguity curve; breakdowns desaturate, choruses re-saturate. That's a second-order harmonic variable your mapping produces for free.
Color kinematics. Velocity of the color trajectory = harmonic rhythm. Acceleration = harmonic drive (pre-choruses accelerate into the chorus in most pop). Jerk spikes = surprise modulations. These become linter rules: MIN_SECTION_COLOR_DELTA (chorus must sit ΔE > threshold from verse), REGIME_STABILITY (color stays within a radius inside a section, catching accidental modulations), COLOR_MONOTONY (rolling ΔE variance floor).
Color scripts as generative spec. Pixar plans films as color scripts before drawing frames. Do the same: the Arranger agent specifies a target color trajectory, the harmony constraint solver composes chords whose mapped colors trace it within tolerance. Also render the whole song as a "harmonic barcode" strip above the spectrogram in your contact sheets. Structure becomes visible at a glance.
Your dissipation constant has a famous cousin: Schroeder integration. RT60, the standard reverb measurement since 1965, is literally reverse-time integration of energy decay. Fit per-band dissipation constants inside your accumulator framework and you are measuring reverb, sustain, and release character through your own theory. This closes the loop on the original "how do we tell an AI about reverb" question: reverb is just slow dissipation, and your model natively senses it.
Builds and drops as phase transitions. Control parameter = energy injection rate; order parameter = spectral/metrical clarity. A filter-swept build accumulates energy without raising full-band "temperature": that is latent heat, and the drop is the phase change that releases it. The hysteresis loop area = payoff magnitude, a single number for "how hard the drop hits." Lint rules: DROP_LATENT_ENERGY (require accumulated potential above threshold before a drop marker), PLATEAU_DETECTOR (energy derivative near zero too long = boredom).
Arrangement as control theory. Once the energy curve is the spec, arrangement decisions (layer entries, filter cutoffs, drum density) are actuators, and the Arranger is a controller tracking a reference trajectory. You inherit the whole vocabulary: overshoot = overhyped build that peaks early, settling time = how fast a section establishes, steady-state error = a chorus that never reaches its target. Solve the inverse problem: given the target curve, schedule the injections.
Cross-band energy flow. Track energy migrating between bands: weight descending from mids into lows entering a drop, rising into highs during a lift. A vector field over (time × frequency) whose divergence marks transitions.
Heat capacity of harmony. Define harmonic entropy per window (distribution over active pitch-class sets), then measure dE/dS: how much energy input it takes to change harmonic disorder. High heat capacity = stable loop sections (choruses absorb hits without changing), low = volatile transition zones. Delightfully weird, plausibly meaningful, definitely publishable.

The general recipe, stated once
For any raw feature x(t): the derivative is motion, the second derivative is force or intent, windowed variance is stability, autocorrelation is periodicity, entropy is predictability, and cross-feature derivatives are coupling. Speech recognition proved this decades ago: MFCC delta and delta-delta coefficients are standard because velocity and acceleration of timbre carry more identity than timbre itself. But the deepest second-order variable is none of these: it's the residual against a predictive model. Meyer and Huron's whole account of musical emotion is expectation play, so "predicted minus actual" is where feel actually lives.
Like-minded concepts to steal
Expectation and information dynamics

IDyOM-style surprisal. Marcus Pearce's model: train a variable-order Markov model on a corpus (or the song's own past), emit per-note surprisal and entropy. Surprisal spikes predict human expectancy ratings and EEG responses. Run it on your IR's MIDI: a melody that never surprises is boring, one that always surprises is noise, and the surprisal trajectory is the hook's shape.
Dubnov's Information Rate. Mutual information between past and present: how much does the song's history predict this moment. IR peaks correlate with listener arousal in empirical studies. This is a single curve that approximates "engagement over time."
Compression distance. LZ-compress the note stream; compression ratio = redundancy = catchiness proxy. Normalized compression distance between sections = similarity without any feature engineering at all.
Entropy rate of chord transitions per genre. Pop sits low, jazz high. A style-conformance number your genre sheets can enforce.

Geometric and force-based theory (your philosophical home turf)

Tymoczko's voice-leading geometry. Chords are points in an orbifold; voice leading is distance. Total semitone displacement per change = voice-leading work; cumulative work per section = harmonic effort. Smoothness becomes a metric, literally.
Lerdahl's tonal tension model. Computed hierarchical tension from pitch-space distance plus attraction values. The closest existing formalization to your thermodynamic instincts, with 20 years of empirical validation behind it.
Larson's musical forces: gravity, magnetism, inertia. Melodies tend to descend (gravity), get pulled toward stable tones with force increasing as they approach (magnetism), and continue patterns (inertia). Fit these forces to a melody; the residual is expressive intent. A melody that obeys all three is smooth; calculated violations are the hooks.
Tonnetz kinematics. Harte's 6-D tonal centroid gives harmony a continuous position; velocity through Tonnetz space = harmonic motion speed, path curvature distinguishes functional progressions (tight orbits) from chromatic wandering (loops and swerves).

Dynamical systems statistics

Recurrence quantification analysis. Embed any feature trajectory in phase space (Takens), build the recurrence plot, compute determinism, laminarity, recurrence rate. Determinism of the groove trajectory is a candidate for the most honest "grooviness" number anyone has proposed.
Oscillator entrainment strength (Large's dynamic attending). Drive a bank of adaptive oscillators with the audio; the phase-locking strength = how hard the music compels entrainment. Groove as a measured pull, not a vibe.
1/f microtiming, the humanness signature. Hennig and colleagues showed human drummers' timing deviations are long-range correlated (1/f), and listeners prefer 1/f humanization over white-noise jitter. Run detrended fluctuation analysis on microtiming: the DFA exponent is a humanness score. Better: generate your humanization with 1/f noise instead of random jitter. Instantly better output, near-zero cost.
Voss and Clarke's 1/f structure. Loudness and pitch fluctuations in music across cultures follow 1/f spectra. Deviation from 1/f in either direction = too predictable or too random. A whole-song sanity check.

Physical motion and effort

Ritardando as physical deceleration. Friberg and Sundberg showed final ritards match the deceleration curves of runners stopping. Fit motion models to tempo and dynamics curves; the residual from natural motion = awkwardness. "Does this phrase brake like a body" is a naturalness lint rule.
Effort integral. Cumulative |dynamics derivative| plus weighted pitch leaps = the physical effort a performer would expend. Sections have effort budgets; a track that demands constant maximum effort exhausts the same way it would exhaust a player.
Danceability band. Energy specifically in the 0.5-4 Hz modulation range, times beat clarity, gated by tempo range. The sensorimotor coupling number.

Cross-stem field dynamics

Co-movement graphs. Correlate stem energy envelopes pairwise over rolling windows: stems as nodes, co-modulation as edges. Graph density = "the band moves together"; density changes at section boundaries measure arrangement cohesion versus independence.
Saliency dominance ratio. Auditory saliency models (Kayser-style) tell you where attention goes. The lead's share of total saliency = mix clarity; saliency entropy across stems = focused versus diffuse arrangement. "The listener doesn't know what to listen to" becomes a number.
Call-and-response detection. Lagged cross-correlation between melodic activity in different stems. Presence and regularity of response structure is a texture-level style feature.
Syncopation as work against the metrical field. Longuet-Higgins syncopation weights violations by metrical position; cumulative syncopation work per bar is a funk quotient. Crucially, Witek's research shows an inverted-U: medium syncopation maximizes groove pleasure. So the linter targets the sweet spot rather than a direction.

Structure-level derivatives

Foote novelty. A checkerboard kernel slid down the self-similarity matrix diagonal yields the novelty curve: second-order structure detection, 25 years old, still the best transition validator. Compare detected novelty peaks against your planned section boundaries; mismatches mean the arrangement isn't articulating its own form.
Composite cadence detector. Cadences are multi-feature convergence events: harmonic clarity rising, tension falling, durations lengthening, register descending, all within a window. The conjunction score is a third-order event detector for "arrival."
Phrase arc grammar. Fit melodic contours to an archetype library (arch, ramp, sigh, wave); the distribution of archetypes per song is a style fingerprint, and archetype sequence grammar catches phrases that end shapeless.
Tonal anchoring drift. Krumhansl key-profile correlation per window gives key clarity; its slow derivative detects drift and pivot moments, marking modulations by how the ground moves rather than by chord labels.

Build order
Cheapest wins first: (1) color trajectory + ΔE in LAB, since your mapping already exists; (2) the Schroeder bridge, fitting per-band dissipation inside your accumulator, which lands reverb sensing; (3) 1/f humanization plus DFA scoring, the single fastest audible quality gain; (4) surprisal via a simple PPM/Markov model on the IR, upgrading to IDyOM later; (5) Witek syncopation targeting; (6) Foote novelty versus planned sections. Items 1, 7, 10, and the color-script-driven solver are the ones I'd flag as original enough to write up.
One last thought you'll enjoy: Zuckerkandl argued in Sound and Symbol that tones are heard as forces, not objects, and that music is the one place we perceive pure dynamic becoming. Your whole framework is a bet that he was right, that the musical fact is the derivative and not the state. The engine is a left-hemisphere apparatus of extraordinary elaborateness built to track exactly the kind of moving, tensed, directional reality the right hemisphere perceives directly. Which is probably why it takes thirty-three numbered items to approximate what your ear does in half a second.
